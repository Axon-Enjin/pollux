Problem statement

Saturday outreach is on the calendar. Mia has a Page and a mandate, and a blank morning: no agenda, no handouts, no link students keep after the tarps come down. A Facebook event is an empty room. A paid vendor workshop prices out the desks that need a session this week. Youth desks outside Manila get the same mandate with even less ready material.

UNESCO's MIL programme asks people to think critically, check sources, use media safely, share ethically, and take part in public talk that is fair. In Philippine barangays, that work is often this Saturday slot: SK and LGU youth desks with a room, a weekend, and no kit.

UNESCO notes that 85% of citizens worry about online disinformation, two-thirds of digital content creators do not systematically fact-check before sharing, and 80% of young people use AI tools multiple times a day. Those figures describe the problem climate. They are not an endorsement of Pollux. Fluent AI text makes rumors look official. SK already has the mandate. They lack a campaign they can run this weekend without a vendor.

Objectives

Modest results. A session this Saturday from the kit. Students keep a URL.

  3. Team delivery. Through 16 Aug 2026, this six-person student team keeps the kit, facilitator path, and English proposal ready for the UNESCO portal. Success: the roster is complete, the HTML matches this text, and the founder can upload.

Prototype or concept

Pollux is the open campaign kit an SK officer runs herself: modules, a program guide, and a site students return to after.

For SK and LGU youth officers who need an outreach or bootcamp this week and do not know where to start, Pollux is the open campaign kit: teachable modules, a run-of-show, logistics, and a site students can keep using. Unlike a blank Facebook event or a paid vendor workshop. Materials stay free.

They run the session. We pack the bag.

The kit already lives in this repo (docs/outreach-kit/). Six modules in fixed order:

  1. Access the pack. Know the source: publisher, date, version.
  2. Pause before share. Think critically before the forward.
  3. Not in pack, not official. Allowlist as session behavior.
  4. AI does not know. Fluency is separate from knowledge.
  5. Ethical share or refuse. A human commits, or refuses.
  6. Act: launch the desk. Optional later walk. The Saturday session still completes on modules 1-5, print, and the student URL.

Facilitators get a 3Ds run-of-show (Document / Demonstrate / Duplicate) for a 45-minute barangay slot or a half-day camp, plus a logistics list they can tailor. Print packet if 3G dies. After the session, she sends students the URL. Seekers Guild partners with SK and LGU on the same materials, including outside Manila. Nothing valuable is paywalled.

MIL as session behavior. MIL here is what the room practices.

  • Think critically: pause before share, practiced in the room.
  • Spot fake news: not in the pack means not official, taught as session behavior.
  • Know the source: publisher, date, version named on the desk or paper card.
  • Use media well: student URL after class; print packet if 3G dies.
  • Share ethically: human commit or refuse; the kit does not auto-post.
  • Build a better society: SK and LGU run the same free kit; Guild partners beyond major hubs.
